# The `b=2` Hall rectangle forces a disjoint-list Kempe core

**Status:** written proof; hash-pinned internal audit GREEN in
[`hc7_k7minus_five_centre_b2_rectangle_locks_audit.md`](hc7_k7minus_five_centre_b2_rectangle_locks_audit.md).
This note sharpens the all-rainbow `b=2` row of the five-centre two-cut
attack.  It does not complete the resulting rooted minor to a `K_7^-`
minor.

Throughout, `K_7^-` denotes `K_7` with one edge deleted.

## 1. Setting

Use the hypotheses and notation of the audited
[global five-root palette alternative](hc7_k7minus_five_centre_t5_global_palette.md).
Assume its all-rainbow outcome and `b=2`.  Thus there are exactly two
pole-incident centres, which we name `z_p,z_q`, with

\[
 z_p p,z_q q\in E(G),\qquad z_p q,z_q p\notin E(G),
\tag{1.1}
\]

and the other three centres have no pole neighbour.  Moreover

\[
 |N_C(z_p)|=|N_C(z_q)|=4,qquad
 |N_D(z_p)|=|N_D(z_q)|=3,                            \tag{1.2}
\]

and each of `N_D(z_p),N_D(z_q)` is a triangle.  Put

\[
 T=(Z-\{z_p,z_q\})\cup\{p,q\},\qquad
 L=C,\qquad R=D\cup\{z_p,z_q\}.                     \tag{1.3}
\]

The set `T` is independent, both closed sides are connected, and the only
`L`--`R` edges are the eight edges from `N_C(z_p)\cup N_C(z_q)` to their
respective centres.

## 2. The exact eight-position Hall obstruction

### Lemma 2.1 (eight-position Hall criterion)

Let `F` be a set of at most eight positions in a `5 by 5` array.  If no
permutation avoids `F`, then `F` contains one of the following:

1. all five positions of one row or one column;
2. a `2 by 4` rectangle; or
3. a `4 by 2` rectangle.

Conversely, each displayed configuration prevents an avoiding permutation.

#### Proof

If the complementary bipartite graph has no perfect matching, Hall's
theorem gives a set of `k` columns with at most `k-1` allowed row
neighbours.  Hence `F` contains a rectangle with `k` columns and at least
`6-k` rows, and

\[
                         |F|\ge k(6-k).              \tag{2.1}
\]

For `k=3`, the lower bound is nine and is impossible.  For
`k=1,2,4,5`, the forced configurations are respectively a full column, a
`4 by 2` rectangle, a `2 by 4` rectangle, and a full row.  The converse is
the same Hall obstruction read directly.  \(\square\)

Apply the contraction-colouring construction of Lemma 1.1 in
[the three-root palette note](hc7_k7minus_five_centre_t3_palette_gluing.md)
to (1.3).  It gives proper six-colourings

\[
 \phi_C\text{ of }G[C\cup T],\qquad
 \phi_R\text{ of }G[D\cup\{z_p,z_q\}\cup T]         \tag{2.2}
\]

in which `T` is monochromatic.  Name its colour `gamma` and put

\[
                         \Omega=[6]-\{\gamma\}.      \tag{2.3}
\]

Every endpoint of one of the eight crossing edges avoids `gamma`.

### Lemma 2.2 (common four-colour rectangle)

Unless `G` is six-colourable, there is a four-set `Q\subseteq\Omega` such
that, for `x\in\{p,q\}`,

\[
                  \phi_C(N_C(z_x))=Q,               \tag{2.4}
\]

and each of the four colours occurs exactly once on each contact set.

#### Proof

Temporarily erase the colours of `z_p,z_q` in `phi_R`.  Each centre has,
inside this closed side, only its incident pole of colour `gamma` and its
three-vertex contact triangle as neighbours.  The triangle is rainbow.
Consequently each centre has at least two available colours in `Omega`.
Choose distinct available colours for `z_p,z_q`.

Form the forbidden-position relation of the contraction-colouring gluing
criterion.  Its positions lie in the two distinct rows named by the two
centre colours, and each row contains at most four positions.  It therefore
contains neither a full row nor a full column.  If it has no avoiding
permutation, Lemma 2.1 forces the `2 by 4` rectangle on those two rows.
Both rows must contain exactly the same four columns.  Since each row is
generated by exactly four crossing edges, the four contact vertices of
each centre have four distinct colours, and their two colour sets agree.
This is (2.4).  An avoiding permutation would instead glue the two closed
side colourings and six-colour `G`.  \(\square\)

## 3. The two centre lists are disjoint

For `x\in\{p,q\}`, put

\[
 T_x=N_D(z_x),\qquad
 L_x=\Omega-\phi_R(T_x).                             \tag{3.1}
\]

The set `L_x` is exactly the set of colours in `Omega` available at `z_x`
after its colour is erased.  In particular,

\[
                         |L_x|\in\{2,3\}.            \tag{3.2}
\]

The value is two when `T_x` avoids `gamma` and three when `T_x` contains
`gamma`.

### Lemma 3.1 (disjoint-list obstruction)

Unless `G` is six-colourable,

\[
                         L_p\cap L_q=\varnothing.    \tag{3.3}
\]

Consequently, up to interchanging the poles,

\[
 (|L_p|,|L_q|)\in\{(2,2),(2,3)\}.                  \tag{3.4}
\]

More explicitly:

1. in the `(2,3)` case, `L_p,L_q` partition `Omega`,

   \[
       \phi_R(T_p)=L_q,\qquad
       \phi_R(T_q)=L_p\cup\{\gamma\};              \tag{3.5}
   \]

2. in the `(2,2)` case, if
   `\Omega-(L_p\cup L_q)=\{h\}`, then

   \[
       \phi_R(T_p)=L_q\cup\{h\},\qquad
       \phi_R(T_q)=L_p\cup\{h\}.                  \tag{3.6}
   \]

#### Proof

If `r\in L_p\cap L_q`, recolour both nonadjacent centres with `r`.  In the
forbidden-position relation, the two rows of Lemma 2.2 collapse to one row,
whose forbidden columns are exactly the four members of `Q`.  This relation
has an avoiding permutation, so the colourings glue and six-colour `G`.
This proves (3.3).

Each list has order two or three and both lie in the five-set `Omega`, so
(3.4) follows.  Since

\[
             \phi_R(T_x)\cap\Omega=\Omega-L_x,      \tag{3.7}
\]

and the triangle has three distinct colours, (3.5)--(3.6) follow by taking
complements in `Omega`.  \(\square\)

## 4. Every cross-list pair is a Kempe connection

Delete `z_p,z_q` from `phi_R` and retain the resulting colouring of
`G[D\cup T]`.  For `a\in L_p`, equation (3.5) or (3.6) gives a unique
vertex

\[
                         t_q^a\in T_q
       \quad\text{with}\quad \phi_R(t_q^a)=a.       \tag{4.1}
\]

Similarly, for `b\in L_q`, let `t_p^b` be the unique vertex of `T_p` of
colour `b`.

### Lemma 4.1 (complete cross-list Kempe family)

For every `a\in L_p` and `b\in L_q`, the vertices `t_q^a,t_p^b` lie in
one common `a`--`b` component of the colouring of `G[D\cup T]`.  Hence
there is a `t_q^a`--`t_p^b` path whose vertices have colours `a,b` and
whose internal vertices all lie in `D`.

#### Proof

Suppose the two vertices lie in different `a`--`b` components.  Interchange
`a,b` on the component containing `t_q^a`.  The boundary `T` remains
monochromatic because its colour is `gamma`.

The colour `b` was absent from `T_q`, and `t_q^a` was its unique
`a`-coloured vertex.  Thus the new available list at `z_q` is

\[
                         (L_q-\{b\})\cup\{a\}.      \tag{4.2}
\]

The triangle `T_p` is unchanged: `a` was absent from it and its unique
`b`-coloured vertex lies in the other component.  Its available list is
still `L_p`.  The two new lists now meet in `a`.

Keep the fixed colouring `phi_C` from Lemma 2.2 and give both centres the
common available colour `a`.  Exactly as in Lemma 3.1, the forbidden
relation has one row and only the four columns in `Q`; an avoiding
permutation glues the two shores.  This contradiction proves the common
component assertion.  Since the path colours are different from `gamma`,
no internal vertex belongs to `T`, so its interior lies in `D`. \(\square\)

## 5. Rooted-minor consequences

We use the Kriesell--Mohr conversion in the exact form already used in the
audited
[degree-seven neighbourhood theorem](../results/hc7_k7minus_degree7_clique_incidence.md):
every graph on five vertices with at most six edges has property `(*)` for
bichromatic connections.  Thus at most six prescribed pairs of a
five-colour transversal can be realized by disjoint connected rooted bags;
literal root edges supply any remaining desired adjacencies.

### Theorem 5.1 (the unequal-list row contains a rooted `K_5`)

If `\{|L_p|,|L_q|\}=\{2,3\}`, then `D` contains a `K_5`-minor model rooted
at the five non-`gamma` vertices of `T_p\cup T_q`.

#### Proof

Orient the notation so that `|L_p|=2` and `|L_q|=3`.  By (3.5), the three
vertices of `T_p` have the colours in `L_q`, while two vertices of `T_q`
have the colours in `L_p`; its third vertex has colour `gamma`.  Delete the
`gamma` colour class.  The five displayed contact vertices are a
transversal of the five remaining colour classes.

Lemma 4.1 supplies all six bichromatic connections between the two roots
from `T_q` and the three roots from `T_p`.  Apply the Kriesell--Mohr theorem
to this `K_{2,3}` demand graph.  It gives five disjoint connected rooted
bags with all six cross-adjacencies.  The literal edge inside the selected
pair in `T_q` and the three literal edges of the triangle `T_p` supply the
other four adjacencies.  The five bags are pairwise adjacent and lie in
`D`.  \(\square\)

### Theorem 5.2 (the equal-list row contains a rooted four-clique core)

If `|L_p|=|L_q|=2`, then `D` contains a rooted `K_4`-minor model on the four
vertices

\[
       \{t_q^a:a\in L_p\}\cup\{t_p^b:b\in L_q\}.   \tag{5.1}
\]

Moreover, adjoining either contact triangle's vertex of the remaining
colour `h` gives five rooted bags with at most two missing
adjacencies, both incident with the `h`-root.

#### Proof

Choose the `h`-coloured vertex of `T_p` as a fifth transversal vertex and
delete the `gamma` colour class.  Apply the Kriesell--Mohr theorem to the
five-vertex demand graph whose only edges are the four pairs between
`L_p` and `L_q`.  Lemma 4.1 supplies those four bichromatic connections.
Discard the bag rooted at the `h`-vertex.  The four retained rooted bags
have all four cross-adjacencies, while the two literal edges inside
`T_p,T_q` supply the two within-pair adjacencies.  They form the asserted
rooted `K_4` model.

If the fifth bag is retained, its root is adjacent to both `L_q` roots in
the triangle `T_p`.  Thus all pairs of the five bags are adjacent except
possibly the two pairs from the `h`-bag to the two `L_p`-rooted bags.
The symmetric statement follows by choosing the `h`-vertex of `T_q`.
\(\square\)

## 6. Exact nonclosure

The `b=2` all-rainbow row is therefore not an unrestricted eight-edge
gluing failure.  Its complete surviving certificate consists of

1. one common four-colour rainbow on both `C`-contact sets;
2. two disjoint centre-availability lists on the opposite shore;
3. every cross-list bichromatic connection in one fixed contraction
   colouring; and
4. either a shore-confined rooted `K_5` minor, or the rooted `K_4` core and
   at-most-two-missing-edge five-bag model of Theorem 5.2.

These conclusions do not yet give a `K_7^-` minor.  In the unequal-list
case the two centres contact complementary `3+2` subsets of the five rooted
bags, rather than both contacting all five.  In the equal-list case the
fifth bag still has two possible missing adjacencies.  Completing either
configuration requires a graph-structural allocation of two additional
branch sets; Hall's theorem and further permutations of the same fixed
colouring do not supply it.
