## 1. The Hall path

Inclusion-minimal deficiency gives a matching from `U-{u}` onto the
two-set `R` for every `u in U`.  It also makes every singleton proper
subfamily nondeficient, so each member of `U` has an `R`-neighbour.  If all
three had only one such neighbour, two would choose the same member of `R`;
that pair could not be matched onto `R`.  Hence some vertex, named `u_0`,
sees both roots.  The other two vertices can be matched onto different
roots after `u_0` is deleted.  Retaining those four incidences gives exactly

```text
u_1-r_0-u_0-r_1-u_2.
```

This deduction uses collective Hall information only.  It does not infer
that any individual member of `U` dominates `W`.

## 2. Coverage by the support generator

After relabelling `W`, the two complementary model bags have fixed shape
`(3,1)` or `(2,2)`.  A connected three-vertex bag has one of the three
labelled two-edge trees; each two-vertex bag has its unique tree.  The
ordinary quotient has at least nine contacts, so one may nominate one of
the ten bag pairs as absent and retain one actual endpoint edge for every
other pair.  This remains valid when the quotient is complete: simply omit
the retained edge for the nominated pair.  Finally, every still-uncovered
vertex of `W` has an actual neighbour in `U` by collective domination, and
one such edge may be retained.  Thus every normalised Hall return contains
at least one graph generated by the program.

I independently reconstructed this process in Python, using sets of 21-bit
edge masks rather than the C recursion.  It reproduced exactly

```text

shape (2,2) retained supports       4032
```

For each support I then independently enumerated the models using only a
six-vertex Hall matching.  Choose the unmatched member of `U`, omit one of
the six matched roots, and initialise the other five bags at their distinct
matched vertices.  The unmatched vertex and the vertex whose root was
omitted can each be assigned to one bag or left unused.  These are all
matching-anchored possibilities.  Direct connectivity and quotient tests
gave

```text

```

in agreement with the frozen verifier.

## 4. Exact rooted predicate and coloured classification

For a selected centre, the verifier gives it both roots `r_0,r_1`, gives
the two Hall leaves one root each, and gives the four vertices of `W` their
four distinct matching roots.  For each omitted root it assigns each of the
seven shore vertices either to one of five root bags or to no bag.  The
`6^7=279936` assignments are exhaustive.  An empty internal part correctly
represents a bag consisting only of its root.  For a nonempty part, the
rooted-connectivity routine starts at every vertex adjacent to the root and
then performs exact internal reachability.  The contact routine checks the
two possible root-to-opposite-part incidences and all internal cross-edges;
there is no omitted root-to-root case because `S` is stable.

There are three possible Hall-path centres for each of the 24
matching-resistant `(2,2)` supports.  The full predicate leaves exactly 24
unrooted coloured pairs.  Its canonicalisation maps the centre to `0`, may
exchange the two Hall leaves together with their root roles, and applies all
`4!` simultaneous permutations of `W` and its four matching roots.  This is
exactly the colour-preserving isomorphism group.  All 24 pairs have eleven
edges and one canonical mask,

## 5. The final contradiction

If no rooted model exists, the extension check forces the whole internal
graph to be the eleven-edge residual.  Its Hall leaves have internal
neighbourhoods

```text
N_C(u_1)={u_0,w_2,w_3},
N_C(u_2)={u_0,w_0,w_1}.
```

Thus each has exactly three neighbours in `C`.  Since
`R=N_S(U)` has order two, neither leaf can have a boundary neighbour outside
`R`, and each has at most two boundary neighbours.  Consequently

```text
|N_C({u_j})|+|N_S({u_j})| <= 3+2=5,
```

contrary to relative six-connectivity for the singleton `{u_j}`.  Extra
boundary incidences cannot repair this inequality because all of them lie in
the two-set `R`.

No Hall, support-coverage, rooted-predicate, canonicalisation, extension or
degree-contradiction gap was found.  The result closes the order-seven
`i=3` Hall case; it does not close `i=2` or the full sparse-six-cut
programme.
