# An adjacent two-block exterior quotient is never target-free

**Status:** computer-assisted finite theorem with an unbounded critical-host
corollary; adjacent independent cold audit GREEN.  The theorem eliminates
every six-connected quotient obtained by contracting a connected
degree-eight exterior to two adjacent blocks.  It does not prove that such a
contraction always exists, and therefore does not prove Conjecture 21 or
`HC_7`.

Write `K_t^-` for `K_t` with one edge deleted.

## Theorem 1 (adjacent-pair quotient census)

Let `J` be a graph on eight vertices with

```text
delta(J)>=3,                 K_6^- is not a minor of J.
```

Add three new vertices `v,x,y`.  Make `v` complete to `J`, add the edge
`xy`, add no edge from `v` to either `x` or `y`, and join `x,y` arbitrarily
to `J`.  Call the resulting graph `Q`.  If `Q` is six-connected, then

```text
                         K_7^- is a minor of Q.
```

### Finite verification

The verifier is
[`experiments/sixconnected_codegree2_two_vertex_verify.py`](experiments/sixconnected_codegree2_two_vertex_verify.py).
From the repository root run

```text
UV_CACHE_DIR=/tmp/hadwiger-uv-cache uv run python -B \
  active/experiments/sixconnected_codegree2_two_vertex_verify.py
```

Every unlabelled eight-vertex graph is generated by adjoining one vertex,
with every possible neighbourhood, to an order-seven graph in NetworkX's
complete graph atlas.  Exact isomorphism testing leaves `2,590` graphs of
minimum degree at least three.  An exact contraction--deletion minor search
leaves `1,562` which have no `K_6^-` minor.

Six-connectivity forces each of `x,y` to have at least five neighbours in
`J`, so each misses at most three vertices.  It also forces a cubic vertex
of `J` to be adjacent to both `x,y`, and a degree-four vertex to be missed
by at most one of them.  Conversely, every attachment satisfying these
necessary degree conditions is included in the census.  Up to interchanging
`x,y`, it gives

```text
degree-feasible attachment pairs       668,408
six-connected quotients                611,678
```

For completeness, the six-connectivity test is exact.  If `v` survives a
cut of order at most five, all surviving vertices of `J` lie in one
component through `v`.  Minimum degree prevents a lone member of `{x,y}`
from being separated.  The adjacent pair can be separated only when its two
members have the same five neighbours in `J`; that case is tested and
rejected.  If `v` is deleted, at most four more vertices are deleted, and
the verifier checks every such deletion in `Q-v` directly.

The exact minor engine starts from singleton bags and recursively either
deletes a bag or merges two touching bags.  Hence it reaches every family
of disjoint connected branch sets: contract a spanning tree in each desired
bag and delete all unused vertices.  At seven bags it accepts exactly when
at most one pair of bags is nonadjacent.  Every returned model is checked
again for disjointness, connectivity and its twenty-one pairwise contacts.

Only `407` distinct model templates are needed, but every cached template is
rechecked in the current labelled quotient before reuse.  The complete run

## Corollary 2 (the exact full-exterior contraction gate)

Let `G` be a seven-connected graph with no `K_7^-` minor, let `v` have
degree eight, and put `J=G[N_G(v)]`.  Suppose

```text
delta(J)>=3,
G-N_G[v] is one connected component C,
N_G(C)=V(J).
```

There is no partition

```text
C=X dot_union Y
```

such that `X,Y` are nonempty and connected, there is an `X-Y` edge, and
the quotient obtained by contracting `X` and `Y` to two vertices is
six-connected.

### Proof

If such a partition existed, contract `X` to `x` and `Y` to `y`.  The
edge between the two parts survives as `xy`; neither quotient vertex is
adjacent to `v`; and `v` remains complete to the eight vertices of `J`.
The local graph `J` has no `K_6^-` minor, since adjoining the singleton
bag `{v}` to such a model would give a `K_7^-` minor in `G`.  The quotient
therefore satisfies Theorem 1.  It contains `K_7^-`, which lifts through
the two contractions to a `K_7^-` minor of `G`, a contradiction.
\(\square\)

## Critical-host consequence

In a hypothetical minor-minimal non-six-colourable `K_7^-`-minor-free
graph, every degree-eight centre has an eight-vertex neighbourhood `J`
with no literal `K_4` and with `alpha(J)=3`.  If all incident codegrees are
at least three, then `delta(J)>=3`; the audited full-exterior reduction
makes `G-N[v]` one connected `J`-full component.  Corollary 2 consequently
applies.

Thus the all-codegree-at-least-three branch has been reduced to a precise
unbounded statement:

> the connected full exterior admits no adjacent connected two-block
> contraction whose quotient remains six-connected.

Proving that every such seven-connected closed shore *does* admit one of
these contractions would force an incident edge of codegree at most two.
By the audited critical separator-or-surplus theorem, its contraction would
then be a six-connected, exactly six-chromatic, target-free graph with
strict coefficient-four surplus.  That contraction-existence statement is
the remaining global theorem; it is not asserted here.

## Scope

Theorem 1 is a complete finite result, and Corollary 2 applies to an
exterior of arbitrary order.  The missing step is structural rather than
computational: contractions of a large connected shore can destroy
six-connectivity.  No claim is made that seven-connectivity of the original
host automatically supplies the required two-block contraction.
