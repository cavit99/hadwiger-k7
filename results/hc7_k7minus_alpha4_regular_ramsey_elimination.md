# The independence-four exceptional-centre branch is impossible

**Status:** written reduction plus computer-assisted finite result; separate
internal audit
[`GREEN`](hc7_k7minus_alpha4_regular_ramsey_elimination_audit.md).  This theorem
eliminates one exhaustive branch inside a hypothetical counterexample.  It
does not by itself prove the `K_7^-` six-colour conjecture or `HC_7`.

## 1. Finite regular Ramsey theorem

### Theorem 1.1

There is no simple graph `J` on 25 vertices satisfying

\[
 d_J(v)=8\quad(v\in V(J)),\qquad
 \alpha(J)=4,\qquad
 \omega(J)\leq4.                                      \tag{1.1}
\]

**Status:** computer-assisted finite result.  Its deterministic standard-
library generator and proof checker driver is
[`hc7_k7minus_alpha4_regular_ramsey_elimination_verify.py`](hc7_k7minus_alpha4_regular_ramsey_elimination_verify.py).

#### Exact finite reduction

Suppose that `J` satisfies (1.1), and fix an independent set

\[
                         U=\{u_0,u_1,u_2,u_3\}.         \tag{1.2}
\]

For every nonempty `S subseteq U`, let

\[
 n_S=\bigl|\{x\in V(J)-U:N_J(x)\cap U=S\}\bigr|.       \tag{1.3}
\]

No mask is empty, since an outside vertex anticomplete to `U` would extend
`U` to an independent five-set.  Regularity gives

\[
 \sum_{\varnothing\ne S\subseteq U}n_S=21,
 \qquad
 \sum_{S\ni u_i}n_S=8\quad(i=0,1,2,3).                \tag{1.4}
\]

For a singleton mask `S={u_i}`, its vertices form a clique: two nonadjacent
members together with `U-{u_i}` would be an independent five-set.  Adjoining
`u_i` to this clique and using `omega(J)<=4` gives

\[
                         0\leq n_{\{u_i\}}\leq3.        \tag{1.5}
\]

The verifier enumerates every nonnegative integral solution of (1.4)--(1.5).
There are exactly 505.  Relabelling the four vertices of `U` leaves exactly
40 orbits.

Fix one orbit representative and label the other 21 vertices by their masks.
Only the 210 adjacencies among those vertices remain unknown.  The CNF for
that representative contains precisely the following constraints.

1. A vertex with mask `S` has exactly `8-|S|` neighbours among the 21
   outside vertices.  Exact cardinalities are encoded by explicit prefix
   threshold gates.
2. For every five-set, at least one of its ten pairs is a nonedge.
3. For every five-set, at least one of its ten pairs is an edge.

The centre-centre nonedges and centre-outside incidences in items 2--3 are
literal constants.  Thus the formula is satisfiable exactly when that orbit
contains an 8-regular graph with neither a clique nor an independent set of
order five.

CaDiCaL 3.0.1 returns `UNSAT` for all 40 formulas and emits one binary DRAT
refutation for each.  The independent `drat-trim` checker at revision
`2e3b2dc0ecf938addbd779d42877b6ed69d9a985` verifies all 40 refutations.
The generated proofs are intentionally not retained in Git: they occupy
hundreds of megabytes, are reproducibly regenerated by the adjacent script,
and are deleted after checking unless an output directory is requested.

The exact generator fingerprints and expected output are recorded in Section
3.  Since every possible incidence orbit is refuted, (1.1) is impossible.
\(\square\)

## 2. Application to the critical host

### Theorem 2.1

Let `G` be a graph such that:

1. every proper minor of `G` is six-colourable;
2. `G` contains no literal `K_5`;
3. the set `B` of degree-eight vertices has order at least 25; and
4. `alpha(G[B])=4`.

Then no such `G` exists.

**Status:** written proof, conditional only on the computer-assisted Theorem
1.1.

#### Proof

Choose an independent four-set `U subseteq B`.  Every vertex of `B-U` has a
neighbour in `U`, by maximality.  For `u in U`, put

\[
 A_u=\{x\in B-U:N_G(x)\cap U=\{u\}\},
 \qquad a=\sum_{u\in U}|A_u|.                          \tag{2.1}
\]

Each `A_u` is a clique: otherwise two nonadjacent members together with
`U-{u}` give an independent five-set in `G[B]`.  The set
`{u} union A_u` is therefore a clique, so the absence of a literal `K_5`
gives `|A_u|<=3` and hence

\[
                              a\leq12.                  \tag{2.2}
\]

Write `b=|B|`.  Counting incidences from `U` into `B-U` gives

\[
 32\geq\sum_{x\in B-U}|N_G(x)\cap U|
    \geq a+2\bigl((b-4)-a\bigr)=2b-8-a.               \tag{2.3}
\]

Consequently `a>=2b-40`.  Combining this with (2.2) yields

\[
                              25\leq b\leq26.           \tag{2.4}
\]

Since every independent set in `G[B]` has order at most four,

\[
                       \chi(G[B])\geq\lceil b/4\rceil=7. \tag{2.5}
\]

If `B` were a proper subset of `V(G)`, the induced graph `G[B]` would be a
proper minor and hence six-colourable, contrary to (2.5).  Thus
`B=V(G)`: the graph is 8-regular and has order `b`.

If `b=26`, then for any vertex `v`, a six-colouring of the proper minor
`G-v` would partition its 25 vertices into six independent sets.  Each has
order at most four, so they cover at most 24 vertices, a contradiction.
Therefore `b=25`.

We have reduced `G` to a 25-vertex 8-regular graph with independence number
four and clique number at most four, contradicting Theorem 1.1.  \(\square\)

### Corollary 2.2

In the critical host for the `K_7^-` six-colour conjecture, the graph induced
by the exceptional degree-eight vertices cannot have independence number
four.  Since that set has at least 25 vertices and contains no `K_5`, the
equality `R(4,5)=R(5,4)=25` then forces an independent set of at least five
exceptional centres.  The Ramsey equality is the main theorem of McKay and
Radziszowski, [*R(4,5) = 25*](https://doi.org/10.1002/jgt.3190190304),
*Journal of Graph Theory* 19 (1995), 309--322.

**Status:** computer-assisted finite result plus written deduction.  The last
sentence uses the established external value `R(4,5)=25`.

## Exact scope

The result eliminates the complete `alpha(G[B])=4` branch and guarantees five
independent exceptional centres in a hypothetical critical host.  It does not
resolve the rooted-model outcome for those five centres, eliminate the exact
separator outcome, synchronize shore colourings, prove the `K_7^-`
six-colour conjecture, or prove `HC_7`.
